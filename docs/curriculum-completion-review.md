The 281 added contextual lessons comprise 111 grammar lessons and 170 vocabulary groups. The full path retains 324 lesson identities, including the 43 original units. Learners encounter vocabulary, complete utterances, grammar, sentence construction and conversations with graded speaker gaps. English and Spanish use the same reviewed language material and INALI display spelling.

A completed entry needs its introduction, a reviewed sentence using the selected meaning, a sentence-construction activity and a meaning-compatible final conversation gap in its own assigned lesson. Bare labels, substring matches, another meaning of the same word and an unrelated lesson do not satisfy that check. Of the 1,286 completed entries, 873 use their exact citation form and 413 use explicitly reviewed forms for the sentence’s person, possession or other documented requirement.

## Remaining vocabulary production holds

These 23 written entries remain available with their reference or usage limits. They are not counted as completed conversation teaching:

- Regional or historical standalone use needs further EHN evidence: **koltik, tliltik, koyotl, koskatl, nawkan, sasaka, sekan, tosan, weltiw, xikipilli, wiptla**.
- Proposed compounds or abstracts lack an independently supported complete form: **awetsi, ehekatepetl, ekwa, mahkatlahtoa, momachtihkakalli, tlahtollotl, tlillotl**.
- The required meaning, possessor or register needs an exact usable construction: **kolli, teotl, notsintlan, tech, tepol**.

A related root, synonym or historical headword cannot establish those precise modern forms. For example, a documented animal synonym does not establish an animal meaning for another modern homograph; a number-related bag word does not establish an 8,000-number sentence. The private review records retain the specific source disagreement for each entry.

## How the material was checked

Original teaching was written around Itzli’s topics and communication goals. Private reference coverage guided the questions to investigate. Selected meanings and grammatical frames were then checked against precise primary-source facts, including the [COERLL Chicontepec course](https://coerll.utexas.edu/coerll/oer/nahuatlahtolli/) and explicitly modern IDIEZ entries in the [University of Oregon dictionary](https://nahuatl.wired-humanities.org/). The Chicontepec print dictionary and online IDIEZ contribution belong to the same source family; they are not counted as two independent datasets.

Separate reviews checked utterances, English/Spanish meaning, actor and recipient, possession, number, register and exact source locators. Publication is tied to the reviewed revisions. Existing lesson membership and earlier task records are preserved. The completion audit found no structural validation errors.

Three learner labels were narrowed to the supported meanings: **machtia** teaches someone; **tlatehteka** prepares and shapes tortillas; **tlahko** is middle or half in the documented measurement use. Their original record identities and source evidence remain intact.

The software checks passed 1,092 tests in 86 files, plus type checking, lint, localization and content/course/audio/image audits. They test behavior and evidence constraints; they do not supply a native-speaker pronunciation review or certify unrestricted fluency. CEFR alignment remains an informal description of tasks and progression.

The optimized local build passed. Browser review caught and fixed duplicate card-child keys that could retain an earlier audio control or missing-recording notice. Type checking, lint and 28 focused tests passed after that fix; the rebuild and actual card-transition regression then passed. The English time lesson was checked through vocabulary, context, grammar, wrong-answer feedback/retry, sentence-tile grading and a successful four-turn conversation. Its Spanish vocabulary, context and grammar stages were also reviewed. No completion result was saved.
