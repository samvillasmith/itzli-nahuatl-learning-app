# Itzli Version 3.0: implementation and completion plan

Learner vocabulary counts updated October 9, 2026: **324 guided lessons, 1,724 distinct words and written forms, and 1,806 meaning entries** after content exclusions. The home page, Version 3 announcement and course-size summary advertise the filtered written-form count. The original 1,760-form archive is retained for provenance and is not the learner total.

The implementation checkpoint below was recorded October 6, 2026. Work is authorized for local review. The written course includes the independently reviewed teaching described below, with explicit remaining evidence holds. Software verification, the optimized local build and the bounded browser review passed. The production database, Vercel configuration and existing learner histories were not changed by this expansion. Media planning and coverage figures below describe that earlier checkpoint; see the subsequent audio release documentation and content assurance report for later work.

## The target and the counts

The target is **1,551 written items from the original inventory**: 524 distinct written forms in the frozen app baseline and 1,027 initially missing inventory forms. Completing unrelated conjugations or additional vocabulary does not substitute for those 1,027 items. The first 89 additions were one batch, not a completion milestone.

October 6 archive checkpoint: **1,551 of 1,551 original inventory items represented**, comprising 1,526 displayed target forms and 25 individually reviewed reference spellings. **No target written items were absent from that archive.** Of the 1,027 initially missing target items, 952 had at least one independently supported intended meaning and **75 retained unresolved intended meanings**; their learner cards taught the independently supported, explicitly scoped meanings. The 1,760 archived app forms include 234 additions outside the target. This records written representation before the later content exclusions, not the current learner count, certification of every book meaning or 1,551 unique dictionary lemmas. Media generation was deferred at that checkpoint.

The current reproducible counts are in [the public vocabulary export](../src/data/v3-verified-lexicon.json). It distinguishes:

- Total distinct app written forms, with repeated placements removed.
- Original target forms present and original target forms still absent.
- Newly present target forms whose selected meaning agrees with the private factual anchor.
- Present forms whose intended additional meaning remains unresolved.
- Useful additions outside the original target.

These are written-item counts. Possessed forms, conjugated forms and lexical headwords remain labeled separately. A certified count of unique dictionary lemmas is unavailable. Course phrase records are a separate, overlapping inventory and must not be added to the vocabulary count.

The item-by-item master ledger and verification queue remain in ignored private research. A matching spelling is retrieval evidence, not verification of every meaning. The four body-source precision holds for chokoxtik, onkak, nemahtli and xaloh illustrate that distinction. Their independently documented senses can be taught while the different target senses remain open.

**281 added lessons have context activities:** all 111 grammar lessons and all 170 vocabulary cohorts. **156 cohorts cover every assigned entry, 14 have partial coverage, and none remain word-only.** The flow introduces vocabulary, presents reviewed utterances, explains grammar, practises recall and sentence construction, and finishes with conversations containing graded speaker gaps. Original sentence models retain their source and regional limitations; they are not claimed native recordings or verbatim dictionary examples. Partial cohorts identify the specific entries whose productive use remains held.

The vocabulary index now searches **1,724 learner-visible written forms across 1,806 meaning entries** and returns learners to their course lesson. Existing V3 links remain compatibility redirects into this path. The strict per-entry audit records meaning-compatible context, sentence construction and final conversation assessment separately; a matching string, bare label or different meaning does not establish completed coverage. The frozen original expansion foundation contains **46 sessions, 37 grammar explanation cards, 219 phrase records and 379 practice tasks** (152 recognition and 227 recall), now rendered inside that same course path. Nine of those sessions and 70 tasks cover person and number, smallness and respectful words, descriptive degree and negation, time references, past processes, human objects, everyday exchanges, self-action versus reciprocal readings, and documented conditional forms. ## Research and publication method

Yan García's *Learn Nahuatl, Second Edition* is a reference and inspiration for the coverage of this expansion. Supplemental reference material has been reviewed and its coverage recorded. Itzli publishes its own teaching explanations and practice activities, alongside specifically credited licensed adaptations. Unclear evidence and incomplete coverage are recorded rather than silently counted as complete.

For each target item:

1. Locate independent primary evidence for its exact form and selected meaning. Check the object/person frame, possession, tense and regional scope where relevant.
2. Compare that fact with the regional references. Record agreement, narrower meaning, homograph, spelling difference or real disagreement. Never force an independent source to agree.
3. Write original brief English and Spanish labels and scope notes. Organize practice around communication goals chosen for Itzli.
4. Review the proposed sense independently, attach precise source locators and hash-linked decisions, and export only approved fields. Hold unresolved facts outside assessed practice.
5. Add source audio only when that recording belongs to the exact item. A recording of a longer sentence does not automatically become an isolated-word recording.

The native-authored [COERLL Chicontepec course](https://coerll.utexas.edu/coerll/oer/nahuatlahtolli/) supports many words, grammatical forms and expressions. Its publisher labels it CC-BY-SA without specifying a license version on the live listing. Itzli's original contributions are offered under CC BY-SA 4.0; source rights and media notices remain distinct.

The [University of Oregon project report](https://blogs.uoregon.edu/wiredhumanitiesprojects/projects/indigenous-language-dictionaries/nahuatl-dictionary-report-for-year-2/) identifies the contributed modern IDIEZ dataset as Eastern Huastecan Nahuatl. Its [online dictionary](https://nahuatl.wired-humanities.org/) also contains historical and other material. Individual fact checks use the explicitly labeled modern IDIEZ fields or explicit Eastern speaker evidence, with the particular community left unspecified when the entry does not establish it. Historical headwords or contributor affiliation alone are insufficient.

The 2016 *Tlahtolxitlauhcayotl: Chicontepec, Veracruz* reference, by John Sullivan and eight named Nahua collaborators, provides another way to check the primary IDIEZ facts at exact PDF pages and headwords. It belongs to the same source family as the online contribution and is not counted as a second independent dataset. Source prose and examples remain private; no open license is inferred from the downloadable PDF. Exact-head retrieval leads are recorded separately from approved meanings and usable standalone forms.

Dictionary references support individual linguistic facts. They do not grant reuse of authored definitions, examples, recordings, pictures or an entire compiled collection. Itzli does not publish a replacement scan or reconstruction of the book, its explanations, exercises or lesson arrangement. Independent checking and original organization reduce risk; they do not provide a guarantee against a claim. The U.S. Copyright Office distinguishes underlying words/systems from authored expression and creative compilation selection/arrangement in [Circular 33](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ33.pdf) and [Circular 14](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ14.pdf). No percentage-based copying rule is used as clearance.

## Grammar coverage and conversation power

The final detailed coverage audit identifies **156 mechanisms: 127 explicitly taught in bounded examples, three partly taught, 11 held for evidence or resolution, two retained as reference only, and 13 regional comparisons excluded from EHN production**. The review covers the indexed reference material rather than claiming that every possible source statement has been independently resolved. The internal audit connects each reviewed mechanism to its source locator, actual lesson/rule/drill and remaining scope.

Sixty-five supplemental lessons now teach noun endings, possession, property and activity nouns, state changes, clause links, emotional predicates, verb class/person contrasts, duration and numerical routines, alongside unspecified objects, identity and posture across time, past imagined actions, purpose/direction, relational space, finite wanted actions, embedded questions, demonstratives, quantified groups, ongoing motion, weather versus personal cold, scoped causative/applicative forms, occupation derivation and go/come command number. Reviewed additions extend these existing lessons and the topic cohorts with specific complete forms and conversations. Regional Tzocohuite forms retain their regional label. The original runner now reaches **all 128 selected grammar production/checkpoint items**, repairing its previous 73-item execution gap. Ten unsupported fixed wanted-action complements were corrected with stable IDs and their stale recordings withheld.

The three partial mechanisms have precise limits: documented base-twenty awareness and forty do not teach unrestricted large-number composition; supported purpose and mental-content clauses with **para** do not establish every reported-speech construction; two auxiliary linking patterns do not establish a complete auxiliary tense paradigm.

The 11 held mechanisms concern community-specific syllable-final **w/kw** realization, connected **tinech-/tech-** contraction, namesake/baptism address customs, the disputed **zceh/zqueh** future plural, optional **i-** in relational expressions, unrestricted morpheme ordering, sudden/recent auxiliary readings, regional **ok** placement, broad claims about 400/8,000 usage, the immediate-sequence **san** construction, and historical directional explanations of lexical verbs. None is presented as a universal EHN rule. The two reference-only mechanisms concern generalizing verbal **-ka** modifiers and abstract-noun adapters into compounds; isolated modeled labels do not supply a productive rule. The 13 regional comparisons remain awareness material rather than assessed EHN speech.

Practice builds from exact forms toward independently verified productive mechanics: who acts and who receives an action, singular versus plural, possession, word order, negation, questions, present/past/future, aspect and derivation within the attested frames. New sentence construction requires approved stems, frames and combinations; a broad text generator must not invent convincing-looking EHN. Completing a bounded example does not approve unrestricted substitutions, every paradigm or community-specific pronunciation. Available activities also do not imply that every sentence appears in a single randomized lesson run.

Build communication around self-expression: introduce oneself, ask follow-up questions, request clarification, express needs and preferences, describe family and location, plan an activity, recount what happened and handle an unexpected reply. Recognition is only one skill. Written recall, delayed review, listening, spoken rehearsal and meaningful exchanges must all contribute to practice. Completion badges and self-reported recordings do not certify conversational fluency.

## Protecting existing learners

The owner's reported 29 production users keep their old course IDs, completion history and established progress stores. Legacy numeric lesson IDs 1–43 remain unchanged. Reviewed V3 course completion IDs retain their existing string identities while their routes become lessons 44–89. Vocabulary lesson IDs and memberships are frozen in the published path manifest; future additions require an explicit append that preserves them. The unified path, continuation and progress display read the existing stores together without replacing them.

The October 9 release update closes the initial device-only gap: signed-in V3 lesson completions, vocabulary practice, vocabulary bookmarks and vocabulary notes sync through an optional versioned envelope in the existing spaced-repetition JSON column. Existing account-scoped browser records upload automatically. Server merges preserve V3 state when an older client omits the envelope, and concurrent practice attempts merge without duplicate counting. Corrupt, future-version or unavailable browser storage is preserved and reported rather than overwritten; failed saves remain pending. Personal expression notebooks and unfinished conversation checkpoints remain device local. Guest and local-review progress do not write to production.

The loopback review launcher sets local review mode. Authentication and progress routes return through that guard before production database access. The local review account is distinct from live sign-in; this preview does not validate live authentication, cloud sync or microphone permission on the learner's device. Production release requires a concrete review of backward compatibility, deployment configuration, recovery and progress behavior.

## CEFR alignment and source credits

Each lesson has a transparent CEFR skill profile with original can-do goals and the related Council of Europe scale families. This is a pedagogical alignment, not official certification or evidence that completing guided exercises establishes spontaneous speaking proficiency. Partially covered cohorts disclose their held entries. Skills and assessment evidence are separate from a topic’s position in the path.

The existing Sources and credits page distinguishes Itzli’s original course arrangement and exercises, licensed COERLL material reused or adapted with attribution, individual dictionary/corpus facts, cultural/art sources, and García’s book as a learning reference and inspiration. It links to information about his book and its purchase listing. It does not falsely claim nothing was reused or every unresolved source claim was verified.

Use the two separate [GCP audio cost audit](audio-generation-cost-audit.md) and [OpenAI image cost audit](image-generation-cost-audit.md), alongside [the current media inventory](version-3-media-expansion.md), generated from the actual exports. Synthetic audio remains labeled as an approximation. The EHN lateral affricate in tl needs separate listening review; spelling t plus l or adding air does not certify that sound. Reject misleading clips before binding them to learning activities. No paid provider call has been executed for this expansion.

Images remain deferred. Written body and bathing vocabulary remains available; its text approval never approves a depiction. Genitals, breasts, buttocks, nude people, and children in bathing or body-exposure situations are prohibited. Bathing compositions must use empty showers, tubs or appropriate bodies of water with no people of any age, including reflections or silhouettes. No reviewed card exception can override these depiction rules. First review existing exact assets and rights against the intended sense, consolidate concepts across inflections, apply exclusions and prefer precise interface cues where a picture would mislead. Then choose model, dimensions and quality, verify official pricing, and quote final concepts times expected attempts. A candidate ceiling is not an approved image count or a purchase commitment.

## Verification and local review

`content:v3:publish` validates source locators, review hashes, exact audio assignments, INALI display, task references and private/public boundaries before exporting. It does not deploy, generate media or touch production progress. The original target ledger must be refreshed after imports.

`verify:v3` passed **1,092 tests in 86 files**, type checking, lint, localization and content/course/audio/image audits plus the media census. The payload regression checks all **281 published blueprints** in both languages using the actual expression/word content builders, every construction answer, the coverage partition and every graded speaker gap. Completion history retains the original result through later revisions; retries and answer reveals cannot improve the first-attempt score or duplicate an assessment attempt. The original 1,551-item inventory, 25 reference spellings and 75 intended-meaning holds remain unchanged. Browser review subsequently found duplicate sibling keys that could leave a previous card's audio or notice on screen. The keys were corrected; type checking, lint and the 28 focused lesson/merge tests passed again, followed by a successful optimized rebuild and a browser regression confirming clean card transitions. Software tests validate behavior and provenance constraints; they do not independently certify language accuracy or pronunciation.

The optimized build ran with the local-review guard, followed by `review:local` at http://127.0.0.1:3000/en/. This publication's browser check covered the time lesson's English vocabulary and sentence cards, wrong-answer feedback and retry, grammar, successful sentence-tile grading and a completed four-turn conversation with two graded gaps. The Spanish vocabulary, sentence and grammar stages were also checked. No lesson completion was saved during this review. Live authentication, cloud sync, device microphone capture and actual listening comparison were not tested; production release still requires the backward-compatibility and configuration review described above.

No evidence-based comparison currently establishes that Itzli is the largest EHN course online. That claim requires comparable independently measured vocabulary, skills and usable conversation coverage across other courses.
