# Review of eighteen glossary-label origins

Reviewed October 7, 2026. The eighteen records identified by the copyright audit now have fresh English and Spanish learner labels authored after reading their cited independent evidence directly. The changes use `src/data/reviewed-vocabulary-labels.json`; the earlier three accuracy corrections remain in place. No book photographs, explanations or exercise instructions, or dictionary example sentences, were copied into these labels.

This is a documented factual review and redraft. It does **not** prove how every older label was originally composed, erase the older Git history, or justify a claim that every element of the course is original: properly credited COERLL material is intentionally licensed and adapted.

## Identity and propagation checks

The overlay preserves all eighteen record IDs, written forms, original source spellings, recordings, source locators and factual review hashes. It does not change the frozen lexical inventory, path membership, learner progress keys or publisher's reviewed research packets.

The card/path tests cover English and Spanish vocabulary cards for all eighteen records, in addition to the three earlier label corrections. Seventeen retain their existing expansion-path assignment; the licensed **mama** anchor remains in the vocabulary catalog without acquiring a duplicate expansion lesson. The tests explicitly retain the male/post-marriage/father-in-law scope of **monti**, the visible-place sense of **nepa**, and the component-based provenance of both hill expressions.

The same factual wording is now propagated through the contextual lessons for **nepa**, **nisokimiki**, **sentiya**, **tepeikxitlan** and **tepetsintlan**. The separate exact-target runtime overlay in `src/data/reviewed-context-labels.json` changes **seven phrase translations, six builder prompts, six speaker cues and four mapped meanings across six existing lessons**. It retains the child's/book's visible position, the speaker's own dirt, the place becoming full of people and the identified stone/hill locations. The hill sentence remains first-person rather than being replaced by a bare citation gloss.

`reviewed-context-labels.ts` requires the exact reviewed bilingual before-value pair or its already-applied after-value pair. It also checks the specific phrase's unchanged Nahuatl, the expected lesson revision, builder/conversation answer identity and mapped sentence identities. A changed or missing target requires another review. Similar wording under another identity is left alone. No global text substitution is used; source locators such as `selectedMeaningEn` and frozen research approvals are untouched.
